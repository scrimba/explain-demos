Two rules to check before you file:

Brokers are not permitted to put the compensation already taxed on your W-2 into the basis they report. An RSU sale can therefore show a gain on the 1099-B that you already paid tax on.

Employers can withhold on vesting shares and bonuses at a flat 22 percent, short of the 32 or 37 percent bracket, and the gap becomes a balance due in April.

What individual tax preparation services cover

Individual tax preparation services mean a CPA or EA prepares your federal and state returns and decides the positions taken on them. That work has four parts:

  • The return itself. Form 1040 and whichever schedules your income requires, from Schedule C to Schedule E to Form 8949.

  • The state side. Each state that taxes the income, plus the allocation between them.

  • The filings beside the 1040. FBAR, Form 8938 and Form 3520, which report assets and gifts and carry their own penalties.

  • The judgment calls. Filing status, cost basis, elections, and what carries forward into next year.

We do not sort receipts. Send business and rental figures summarized, and where no summary exists yet, we do the bookkeeping first. We prepare US returns only, so a preparer in that country handles an HMRC or CRA return.

We prepare returns for clients in all 50 states, and the same preparer keeps your file year to year. We carry your carryforwards and depreciation schedules into each new year, wherever you live.

Who we prepare individual returns for

  • Employees with equity.

    RSUs, ESPP, ISOs and NSOs, where the reported cost basis is the problem. Exercise an ISO and hold the shares past December 31, and the IRS counts the spread as AMT income with no sale and no cash. See IPO tax strategy where a liquidity event is involved.

  • People who moved states.

    Moved between grant and vest? The state you left can tax the part of the vest that tracks your workdays there. New York can reach the whole vest when your employer is in New York and you worked elsewhere for your own convenience.

  • Freelancers and single member LLCs.

    Schedule C, the home office and vehicle deductions, and the self employment tax on the same income.

  • Landlords.

    Schedule E per property, depreciation on Form 4562, and the passive loss limits reported on Form 8582.

  • Crypto holders.

    We build Form 8949 from an aggregator report, or reconcile it from raw exchange exports where no report exists. Detail on crypto tax services.

  • Couples deciding how to file.

    We model joint and both separate returns, then recommend one with the arithmetic attached.

  • Americans abroad and foreign nationals here.

    The exclusion or the foreign tax credit, FBAR, Form 8938, and whether the year is a 1040 or a 1040-NR. Handled with international tax.

High net worth tax preparation is a coordination problem. A tax accountant for individuals with equity, a rental, a K-1 and a state allocation has to work them together, because the same numbers appear on more than one form.

CPA vs TurboTax, and when people switch

TurboTax is built for simple returns and does that job well. Many returns need nothing more.

The return changes. Equity vests, a rental closes, you move states mid-year, an account opens abroad. At that point the question is which position to take.

The RSU double-tax trap on your 1099-B

Why an RSU sale can show a gain you already paid tax on, and how code B on Form 8949 fixes it.

RSU cost basis your broker is not allowed to report

When shares vest, your employer puts the value on your W-2 and the IRS taxes it as wages. When you sell, the broker reports a basis without the compensation you already paid tax on, per the Form 8949 instructions.

  • 1,000 shares vest at $60. Your employer reports $60,000 on your W-2.

  • You sell the same day. The 1099-B shows a basis of zero and a $60,000 gain.

  • We correct the basis on Form 8949 with code B. The gain is zero.

The broker’s form is correct as filed, so no error shows. We check each equity sale against the vesting or exercise record before we file.

Three tax thresholds: $1,000, $10,000, $100,000

The three numbers that carry their own IRS penalties, whether or not you owe tax.

Three thresholds with their own penalties: $1,000, $10,000 and $100,000

The IRS attaches a separate penalty to three numbers, whether or not you owe any tax:

None of them depends on whether you owe tax for the year.

  1. 1

    $1,000 owed after withholding.

    The estimated tax penalty applies unless you paid 90 percent of this year or 100 percent of last year, and that becomes 110 percent where last year’s AGI topped $150,000.

  2. 2

    More than $10,000 across foreign accounts.

    An FBAR on FinCEN Form 114, due April 15 and automatically extended to October 15. A report with no tax attached.

  3. 3

    $100,000 gifted by a foreign relative.

    Form 3520, where the IRS charges 5 percent of the gift per month, up to 25 percent, on money that is not taxable income to you.

Sources: IRC 6654, FinCEN Form 114, and IRC 6039F.

The IRS charges 5 percent of the unpaid tax per month for filing late, up to 25 percent, so take the extension to file. It does not extend the time to pay.
